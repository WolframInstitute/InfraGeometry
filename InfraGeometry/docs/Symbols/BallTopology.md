## Usage

<code>[BallTopology]()[*g*, *r*]</code> gives the topology on the vertices of *g* whose closed sets are generated by the balls of radius *r*, as a digraph with an edge *q* → *p* when *Bᵣ(p) ⊆ Bᵣ(q)*.

## Details & Options

The balls *Bᵣ(v) = {u : d(u, v) ≤ r}* are a subbasis of the closed sets. The closure of a vertex is the intersection of the balls that contain it, *cl(p) = {q : Bᵣ(p) ⊆ Bᵣ(q)}*.

A finite topology is the same as its specialization preorder, *q ≤ p* when *q ∈ cl(p)*. The digraph has an edge *q* → *p* for *q ≤ p*, *q ≠ p*, with the edges implied by transitivity removed. A closed set is a set closed under going down the edges; an open set, under going up. Vertices with equal balls lie on one directed cycle.

The digraph has every vertex of *g*, so it carries the whole topology. [TopologicalClosure](), [TopologicalInterior](), [TopologicalBoundary](), [TopologicalNeighborhood]() and [ContinuousMapQ]() take it, and [TopologyGraph]() draws it.

At radius 0 the topology is discrete. From the diameter of a connected graph on it is indiscrete. In between it gets coarser as *r* grows: *Bᵣ₊₁(v)* is the set of vertices within distance 1 of *Bᵣ(v)*, so *Bᵣ(p) ⊆ Bᵣ(q)* implies *Bᵣ₊₁(p) ⊆ Bᵣ₊₁(q)*.

On *Z²* no ball lies in another ball of the same radius. On a patch of the grid every edge comes from the rim, and a vertex far from the rim is open and closed. A vertex of degree 1 has its ball inside the ball of its neighbour at every radius.

| Option | Default | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `"Dual"` | `False` | `True` reverses the digraph: the dual topology, whose open sets are the closed sets of this one |

## Basic Examples

The ball topology of radius 2 on the square grid and on the square mesh. An arrow *q* → *p* says that the ball at *q* contains the ball at *p*. The arrows stay near the rim:

```wl
GraphicsRow @ Table[
  With[{g = InfraSubstrate[name, "Small", "KeepCoordinates" -> True]}, TopologyGraph[g, BallTopology[g, 2]]],
  {name, {"SquareGridGraph", "SquareMeshGraph"}}]
```

At radius 1 on the grid the only arrows go from a rim vertex to its neighbours of degree 1:

```wl
With[{g = InfraSubstrate["SquareGridGraph", "Small"]}, {ends = Select[VertexList[g], VertexDegree[g, #] == 1 &]},
  Sort @ EdgeList @ BallTopology[g, 1] === Sort[DirectedEdge[First @ AdjacencyList[g, #], #] & /@ ends]]
```

On a path the balls shrink towards each end. At radius 2 each end carries a chain, and the middle vertex is isolated:

```wl
EdgeList @ BallTopology[PathGraph[Range[7]], 2]
```

## Scope

At radius 0 there are no edges. At the diameter 16 of the grid all balls are the whole grid, and the 96 edges form one directed cycle through the 96 vertices:

```wl
With[{g = InfraSubstrate["SquareGridGraph", "Small"]},
  {EdgeCount @ BallTopology[g, 0], EdgeCount @ BallTopology[g, 16], VertexCount[g],
   ConnectedGraphQ @ BallTopology[g, 16]}]
```

## Options

### Dual

`"Dual" -> True` reverses every arrow:

```wl
With[{g = InfraSubstrate["SquareMeshGraph", "Small", "KeepCoordinates" -> True]},
  GraphicsRow @ {TopologyGraph[g, BallTopology[g, 2]], TopologyGraph[g, BallTopology[g, 2, "Dual" -> True]]}]
```

## Properties and Relations

The topology gets coarser with the radius: every comparable pair at radius *r* is comparable at *r* + 1:

```wl
With[{g = InfraSubstrate["SquareGridGraph", "Small"]},
  {pairs = Table[EdgeList @ TransitiveClosureGraph @ BallTopology[g, r], {r, 0, 16}]},
  And @@ MapThread[SubsetQ, {Rest[pairs], Most[pairs]}]]
```

The share of ordered pairs of distinct vertices that are comparable rises to 1 at the diameter:

```wl
ListLinePlot[
  Table[With[{g = InfraSubstrate[name, "Small"]}, {n = VertexCount[g]},
    Table[{r, N[EdgeCount[TransitiveClosureGraph @ BallTopology[g, r]] / (n (n - 1))]}, {r, 0, GraphDiameter[g]}]],
    {name, {"SquareGridGraph", "SquareMeshGraph"}}],
  PlotMarkers -> Automatic, PlotLegends -> {"SquareGridGraph", "SquareMeshGraph"}]
```
