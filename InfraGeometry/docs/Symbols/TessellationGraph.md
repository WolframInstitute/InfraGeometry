## Usage

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}]</code> gives the graph of the smallest regular map of type {*p*, *q*}: a closed surface cut into *p*-gons, *q* at every vertex.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}, *k*]</code> gives the *k*-th map of that type in increasing order of size.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}, {*m*, *n*}]</code> gives the *m* × *n* flat torus of a Euclidean type.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}, *G*]</code> gives the map carried by a (2, *p*, *q*)-generation of the finite group *G*.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*f₁*, …, *fₖ*}, *k*]</code> gives the uniform map with the vertex configuration {*f₁*, …, *fₖ*}, built on the *k*-th map of its parent type.

## Details & Options

A regular map has a symmetry taking any directed edge to any other. The graph is its 1-skeleton.

The sign of the [TessellationCurvature]() decides the surface:

| (*p* − 2)(*q* − 2) | Surface | Maps |
|---|---|---|
| < 4 | sphere | the five Platonic solids, one of each type |
| = 4 | flat torus | the *k* × *k* torus for {4, 4}, {3, 6}, {6, 3}, as [TorusTessellation]() |
| > 4 | surface of genus ≥ 2 | quotients of the hyperbolic tiling, in increasing order of size |

The maps of a hyperbolic type are the finite quotients *G* of the triangle group, generated by *x* of order *p*, *y* of order *q* and *xy* of order 2. The map of *G* has *|G|/q* vertices, *|G|/2* edges and *|G|/p* faces. Quotients up to the order 1100 are enumerated; a quotient whose skeleton has a loop or a multiple edge is skipped. Each normal subgroup is one map, so a chiral pair counts twice.

A uniform map is a rectification, truncation or both of a regular map. Snub solids and snub and elongated tilings are not built.

Every map is closed: a circle on a small map wraps round within a few steps. Patches of the tilings are [TessellationNeighborhoodGraph]().

| Option | Default | Values |
|---|---|---|
| [Method]() | [Automatic]() | `"PSL2"`: the *k*-th group PSL(2, ℓ), ℓ prime < 50, with a (2, *p*, *q*)-generation; a fast way to a large map, not in order of size |

[Graph]() options are passed on.

## Basic Examples

The circle of radius 1 about a vertex is a *q*-cycle. On the icosahedron, on the 6 × 6 triangulated torus and on the Klein quartic it has 5, 6 and 7 vertices:

```wl
GraphicsRow @ Table[
  With[{map = TessellationGraph @@ spec}, {hub = First @ VertexList[map]},
    InfraSubstrateHighlight[map, {InfraCircle[hub, 1], hub}, PlotLabel -> First[spec]]],
  {spec, {{{3, 5}}, {{3, 6}, 6}, {{3, 7}}}}]
```

Vertices, edges, faces and Euler characteristic of the Platonic solids, three flat tori and two hyperbolic maps:

```wl
Grid[Prepend[Table[
    With[{map = TessellationGraph @@ spec}, {v = VertexCount[map], e = EdgeCount[map]}, {f = 2 e/spec[[1, 1]]},
      {spec[[1]], v, e, f, v - e + f}],
    {spec, {{{3, 3}}, {{3, 4}}, {{4, 3}}, {{3, 5}}, {{5, 3}}, {{4, 4}, 6}, {{3, 6}, 6}, {{6, 3}, 6},
      {{3, 7}}, {{7, 3}}}}],
  {"type", "V", "E", "F", "χ"}], Alignment -> Left]
```

## Scope

Flat tori of any shape, here 6 × 4:

```wl
GraphicsRow @ Table[TessellationGraph[spec, {6, 4}], {spec, {{4, 4}, {3, 6}, {6, 3}}}]
```

The map of a group: the alternating group on five letters carries the icosahedron:

```wl
IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{3, 5}, AlternatingGroup[5]], PolyhedronData["Icosahedron", "SkeletonGraph"]]
```

Uniform maps. The configuration {4, 6, 8} is the great rhombicuboctahedron, {4, 4, 5} the pentagonal prism:

```wl
{IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{4, 6, 8}], PolyhedronData["GreatRhombicuboctahedron", "SkeletonGraph"]],
 IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{4, 4, 5}], PolyhedronData[{"Prism", 5}, "SkeletonGraph"]]}
```

A hyperbolic uniform map: the truncation {5, 10, 10} of the first map {5, 5} has 60 vertices and keeps the genus 4:

```wl
With[{map = TessellationGraph[{5, 10, 10}]},
  Graph[map, PlotLabel -> {VertexCount[map], TessellationGenus[map, {5, 10, 10}]}, ImageSize -> Medium]]
```

## Options

### Method

The second map {3, 7} has 72 vertices. The second group PSL(2, ℓ) with a (2, 3, 7)-generation is PSL(2, 13), of order 1092, and gives 156:

```wl
VertexCount /@ {TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, 2], TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, 2, Method -> "PSL2"]}
```

## Properties and Relations

The map of a group *G* has *|G|/q* vertices. The first five maps {3, 7} have the orders 168, 504 and three times 1092, the Hurwitz groups of genus 3, 7 and 14:

```wl
7 VertexCount[TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, #]] & /@ Range[5]
```

## Possible Issues

The sphere carries one map of each type, the enumeration stops at the order 1100, and the snub solids are not built. These calls stay unevaluated:

```wl
{TessellationGraph[{3, 5}, 2], TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, 6], TessellationGraph[{3, 3, 3, 3, 4}]}
```
