## Usage

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}]</code> is the smallest regular map of type {*p*, *q*}, as a graph: *p*-gon faces, *q* of them at every vertex.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}, *k*]</code> is the *k*-th map of that type in increasing order of size: the *k* × *k* flat torus when the type is Euclidean, the *k*-th quotient of the triangle group otherwise.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}, {*m*, *n*}]</code> is the *m* × *n* flat torus of a Euclidean type.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[{*p*, *q*}, *G*]</code> is the map carried by a (2, *p*, *q*)-generation of the finite group *G*.

<code>[TessellationGraph]()[*config*, *k*]</code> is the uniform map with vertex configuration *config*, the cyclic list of face sizes around a vertex, built on the *k*-th map of its parent type.

## Details & Options

A regular map of type {*p*, *q*} is a closed surface cut into *p*-gons, *q* at every vertex, with a rotation taking any directed edge to any other. The graph is its 1-skeleton. The faces are not returned.

The sign of (*p* − 2)(*q* − 2) − 4 is the sign of the curvature. It decides the surface:

| (*p* − 2)(*q* − 2) | Surface | Maps |
|---|---|---|
| < 4 | sphere | the five Platonic solids, one of each type |
| = 4 | flat torus | {4, 4}, {3, 6} and {6, 3}, one per size |
| > 4 | hyperbolic surface of higher genus | quotients of the hyperbolic tiling, infinitely many of each type |

The regular maps of type {*p*, *q*} are the quotients *G* of the triangle group by its normal subgroups of finite index. The triangle group is generated by *x* of order *p*, which turns a face, and *y* of order *q*, which turns a vertex, with *x* *y* of order 2, which reverses an edge. The map of *G* has |*G*|/*q* vertices, |*G*|/2 edges and |*G*|/*p* faces. So the maps of a type in increasing order of size are its normal subgroups in increasing order of index, and the *k*-th map is the *k*-th of them. They are found by an enumeration of the normal subgroups up to the order 1100; a type with fewer than *k* maps up to that order leaves the call unevaluated.

Two conventions fix the count. Each normal subgroup is one map, so a chiral pair counts twice, and a map and its dual are distinct unless the map is self-dual. A quotient whose skeleton has a loop or a multiple edge is skipped, so every map is a simple graph: the {3, 8} map of order 48, on the Bolza surface, has 6 vertices and 24 edges and is left out.

The Euler characteristic *V* − *E* + *F* with *F* = 2*E*/*p* reads the surface off the graph: 2 on the sphere, 0 on the torus, negative on a hyperbolic surface.

Every map here is **closed**. A line or a circle drawn on it wraps round the surface, and on a small map the wrap is felt within a few steps. For plane geometry use the patches of [InfraSubstrate](), which are cut from the infinite tiling and have no wrap.

The flat tori are [TorusTessellation]() graphs. A uniform map is built by the Conway operations rectify and truncate, and their composites, on the *k*-th map of its parent type, using the faces of that map: the orbits of the face rotation on a quotient, the lattice cells on a torus. The eleven Archimedean solids that these operations reach are computed from the Platonic solids; the prisms and antiprisms are built directly. The two snub solids, the snub and elongated tilings, and a torus too small to carry a simple map stay unevaluated.

Options:

| Option | Default | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `Method` | `Automatic` | `"PSL2"` |

`Automatic` takes the enumeration of the normal subgroups. `"PSL2"` takes the quotient PSL(2, ℓ) of the *k*-th prime ℓ < 50 whose group has a (2, *p*, *q*)-generation: a fast way to a large map, not in order of size. `Graph` options are passed on to the graph.

## Basic Examples

The circle of radius 1 about a vertex is its link, a *q*-cycle. On the icosahedron it has 5 vertices, on the triangulated torus 6, on the Klein quartic 7: positive, zero and negative curvature.

```wl
Row[Table[
   With[
     {map = TessellationGraph @@ spec},
     {hub = First @ VertexList[map]},
     Labeled[
       InfraSubstrateHighlight[map, {InfraCircle[hub, 1], hub}, ImageSize -> 200],
       First[spec]]],
   {spec, {{{3, 5}, 1}, {{3, 6}, 6}, {{3, 7}, 1}}}]]
```

Vertices, edges, faces and Euler characteristic of the five Platonic solids, three flat tori and two hyperbolic maps.

```wl
Grid[Prepend[
   Table[
     With[
       {map = TessellationGraph @@ spec},
       {nVerts = VertexCount[map], nEdges = EdgeCount[map], nFaces = 2 EdgeCount[map] / First[First[spec]]},
       {First[spec], nVerts, nEdges, nFaces, nVerts - nEdges + nFaces}],
     {spec, {{{3, 3}, 1}, {{3, 4}, 1}, {{4, 3}, 1}, {{3, 5}, 1}, {{5, 3}, 1},
       {{4, 4}, 6}, {{3, 6}, 6}, {{6, 3}, 6}, {{3, 7}, 1}, {{7, 3}, 1}}}],
   {"type", "V", "E", "F", "χ"}], Alignment -> Left]
```

## Scope

The first three {3, 7} maps, of the groups of order 168, 504 and 1092: their vertices and their genus 1 − χ/2.

```wl
Table[
  With[
    {map = TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, k]},
    {chi = VertexCount[map] - EdgeCount[map] + 2 EdgeCount[map] / 3},
    {VertexCount[map], 1 - chi / 2}],
  {k, 3}]
```

A flat torus of any shape.

```wl
VertexCount /@ {TessellationGraph[{4, 4}, {6, 4}], TessellationGraph[{3, 6}, {6, 4}], TessellationGraph[{6, 3}, {6, 4}]}
```

The map of a group: the alternating group on five letters carries the icosahedron.

```wl
IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{3, 5}, AlternatingGroup[5]], PolyhedronData["Icosahedron", "SkeletonGraph"]]
```

The prime family. The second {3, 7} map has order 504; the second quotient PSL(2, ℓ) is PSL(2, 13), of order 1092.

```wl
VertexCount /@ {TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, 2], TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, 2, Method -> "PSL2"]}
```

Uniform maps. The configuration {4, 6, 8} is the great rhombicuboctahedron, and {4, 4, 5} is the pentagonal prism.

```wl
{IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{4, 6, 8}], PolyhedronData["GreatRhombicuboctahedron", "SkeletonGraph"]],
 IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{4, 4, 5}], PolyhedronData[{"Prism", 5}, "SkeletonGraph"]]}
```

A hyperbolic uniform map: the truncation of the smallest {5, 5} map, of genus 4, has pentagons and decagons on 60 vertices and keeps the genus.

```wl
With[
  {map = TessellationGraph[{5, 10, 10}]},
  {VertexCount[map], TessellationGenus[map, {5, 10, 10}]}]
```

## Properties and Relations

A map of type {*p*, *q*} is *q*-regular.

```wl
Union @ VertexDegree @ TessellationGraph[{3, 7}]
```

The map of a group of order |*G*| has |*G*|/*q* vertices. The first five {3, 7} maps have the orders 168, 504 and three times 1092: three normal subgroups of the same index.

```wl
7 VertexCount[TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, #]] & /@ Range[5]
```

The Euclidean types are the tori of [TorusTessellation]().

```wl
IsomorphicGraphQ[TessellationGraph[{3, 6}, {8, 5}], TorusTessellation[{8, 5}, "Triangular"]]
```

## Possible Issues

The sixth {3, 7} map has order 1344, past the order 1100 that the enumeration reaches, and the call stays unevaluated.

```wl
TessellationGraph[{3, 7}, 6]
```

The sphere carries one map of each type.

```wl
TessellationGraph[{3, 5}, 2]
```

The snub solids are not built by rectify and truncate, and the call stays unevaluated.

```wl
TessellationGraph[{3, 3, 3, 3, 4}]
```
